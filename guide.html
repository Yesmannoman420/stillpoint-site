A night of darts, step by step.

Setting up the phone, scoring, tournaments, pairs and the pub TV: everything Still Point does, in the order you meet it.

The camera

  • Placement. Put the phone to one side of the board at about the height of the bull, angled toward it, so the board looks like an oval. A phone stand or a shelf works; it must not move during play.
  • Framing. In Camera setup the whole double ring should be inside the picture. Use zoom if the board is small. The status turns to “Board found” when the outline follows the double ring.
  • Light. Even light on the board helps; avoid strong light from behind the board into the lens.
  • Corrections. Tap a dart to change it. Undo and redo are always there.

Scoring by hand

Above the keys, Darts, Total and Camera switch how the visit is entered, at any moment of a match. The phone remembers whether you prefer Darts or Total.

  • Darts. Single, double or triple, then the number. Miss counts a dart that missed the board.
  • Total. Type the visit's score on the numpad and press OK. A total that busts turns OK into Bust; a total that finishes the leg asks only how many darts it took.
  • Camera. Moves the match to the camera mid-visit: the board is found first, then play carries on with the darts already thrown. Darts or Total moves it back.

The scoring screen fits the phone without scrolling: the keys and the visit controls keep their size and the score takes the rest.

Players and pairs

  • Adding a player. Type a name. With Save to Players on, they keep their averages and history together; off, they play this match only. The Add button, the keyboard's Done key, or closing the sheet with a name typed all add them.
  • Pairs. With four, six or eight people in X01 or cricket, Play in pairs makes teams of two in list order: 1 & 2 against 3 & 4. Each pair keeps one score; partners take turns, one visit each, also across legs. The phone and the TV show who is throwing and who is next.
  • Each partner's numbers. After a pairs match the result shows every partner's own average (marks per round in cricket), best visit and checkout, and who checked out each leg. A saved player's page counts their pairs matches in its own In pairs block.

A second phone

An old phone makes a great camera. On the main phone choose Use companion device in Camera setup; on the second phone tap Companion on Home and scan the code (or accept the request). Then pick which phone films the board. The phones stay paired, also after restarting, until you unpair them. Both need the same Wi-Fi or one phone's hotspot.

If you start a new game on the scoreboard phone while the camera phone still has a match in progress, it asks first.

Tournaments

  • Formats. Knockout (seeded, byes go to the top seeds), Double elimination (lose once and you play on in the losers bracket; the grand final can be reset), League (everyone plays everyone, once or home and away) and Groups then knockout.
  • Matches. X01: 301, 501 or 701, legs and sets, double out; a longer final and semi-finals are one setting, a third-place match another. Cricket or cricket without points: one game per match.
  • Pairs. Play in pairs turns the players into teams of two, as listed or with the partners drawn at random. Saved players keep their pairs results on their own page.
  • Playing. Up next lists the matches that can start. Tap one to play it with the camera, by hand, or to enter a result played elsewhere. Matches played in the app add averages, 180s, checkouts and best legs to the Leaders tab.
  • Corrections. Correct or clear any result. If a different player wins, later results that depended on it stop counting and those matches are ready to play again; nothing is silently thrown away.
  • Tables. Two points per win; ties are broken by leg difference, legs won, the match between the tied players, average and finally seed.
  • Withdrawals. A player who has to leave can be withdrawn; they concede the matches they have not played yet.
  • Places and the Order of Merit. Places earn points (10 for a title, 7 runner-up, 5 third, 4 fourth, 3 for the last eight, 2 for the last sixteen, 1 for playing) that add up in the Order of Merit, all time, this year or the last 90 days.
  • Run it again. A finished tournament starts its next edition with the same rules and players, seeded by where everyone finished.

A phone at every board

  • The desk. In a tournament open Boards and show the venue code. The desk sends every board its next match by itself (or one by one), calls each match out loud, and records each result with its statistics.
  • The boards. On each board's phone choose Tournaments › Join as a board, pick its number and scan the code. The board shows its next match; play it with the camera or by hand, and the result goes back to the desk.
  • Changes of plan. Take a match back from a board from its sheet on the desk. If the Wi-Fi drops, boards reconnect by themselves and a finished result waits on the board until the desk has it.

The TV

  1. Tap the TV icon on the live board or in a tournament, then Start big screen, or pick a Chromecast or Google TV under Chromecast.
  2. On any other TV, projector, laptop or tablet, open the browser and type the address shown (for example 192.168.1.23:8217/s/k3m9qz7), or scan the QR code.
  3. The screen follows the night: the match while one is played, with a card for each leg; with two or more boards playing, every board side by side with who is up next and how long the night still runs; a full-screen call whenever a match goes to a board; and after a tournament the champion, the final places, the Order of Merit and where everyone's darts went.
  • Calls on the TV. The TV speaks the calls through its own speakers. As soon as it has, the phone stops calling so the room hears one voice; a TV that can't speak never silences the calls.
  • Sounds. Big screen › Sounds on the TV takes the club's own walk-on, 180, game shot and finish sounds, and every saved player can have their own walk-on. A Chromecast plays files up to 1.5 MB.
  • With several boards. Every board (the default) or This board, to keep the TV on the match of the phone you hold.
  • Follow on your phone. Players scan the code on the TV to follow the night on their own phone, with their own next match.

Nothing appears? The TV and the phone must be on the same network. Guest Wi-Fi often keeps devices apart; use the main network, or turn on the phone's hotspot and connect the TV to it. Very old TV browsers that cannot keep a live connection check for news every second instead; that is expected.

After a match

The result opens on the scoreline and a stat sheet that sets the players side by side: 3-dart average, first 9, checkout rate, high out, best leg, best visit, 180s, 140+, 100+ and 60+. Below it, every leg: who took it, in how many darts and with what checkout. Report opens the same match with each player's visits, a dart map and finishing. Share the result as a picture, or after a tournament the whole night.

Progress and players

  • Progress. Pick a player and a game: the average and its trend, first 9, checkout rate, wins, best leg, high out and 180s, form, how your visits fall into scoring bands, and personal bests that open the match they came from.
  • Player pages. A saved player's page shows their career: average, best match, best leg, high out, 180s, recent form, how they fare against the people they play most, their pairs record and the tournaments they won or finished on the podium.
  • Home. When the phone knows who you are, the Progress tile shows your average and your last five results.

Practice

Around the Clock, Checkout practice, the Scoring trainer, Grouping on treble 20 and the Bull challenge each have their own result. After a session you see how it compares with your earlier ones: a personal best, or your best and average before.

Accounts

Accounts are optional. Sign in with Google or email to back up the matches you choose, and to approve a match you played on a friend's phone so it counts for you. See Privacy for exactly what is kept.
