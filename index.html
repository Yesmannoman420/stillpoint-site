Camera scoring

Throw. It already knows.

Lean the phone against the wall at an angle, frame the board in the oval guide, and play. Every dart is placed on the board and scored as it lands, with the route to finish right beside the score. Wrong call? One tap fixes it. Prefer to type? The keypad is always one tap away. When the match is over, a stat sheet sets the players side by side: average, first 9, checkouts, best leg, leg by leg.

Everything for a darts night

One phone. The whole evening.

Tournaments

Knockouts, double elimination, leagues and groups into a knockout, for 2 to 64 players and up to 8 boards, in X01 or cricket. Seeded draws, byes, a longer final, a third-place match. Results flow into the bracket on their own; every finish earns points in your club's Order of Merit. Correct a result and the matches that depended on it are ready to play again.

Two phones, one board

An old phone films the board while yours keeps the score in your hand. Pair once with a code and they stay paired, game after game.

Every board its own phone

Running more boards? One phone becomes the desk; a phone at each board scans its code. The desk sends every board its next match and calls it out, and results come straight back into the bracket.

Practice that measures

Around the Clock, checkout and scoring drills, grouping on treble 20, bull challenges, each compared with your own best. Progress and your player page keep your average, form, personal bests and rivals, honestly.

Still Point Live

The match, on the big screen.

Open one address on the TV, a projector or a laptop and the room sees what you see: huge scores, the darts of this visit, the checkout, and who took each leg. When a match goes to a board, the screen calls it. Between matches it shows who is on which board, who is next, the bracket, the champion and the club's Order of Merit. It runs over your own Wi-Fi or the phone's hotspot. Nothing to install on the TV.

A tournament night

From names to a champion.

  1. Add the playersType names one after another, or pick saved players. Draw at random or seed them.
  2. Call the matchesUp next shows who plays on which board. Score with the camera, by hand, or just enter the result. With a phone at every board, the desk sends each one its match.
  3. Put it on the TVStart the big screen and open its address on a browser on the same Wi-Fi.
  4. Crown the championThe bracket fills in, final places earn Order of Merit points, and you can share the results. Next week, run it again.

Private by design

Your darts stay on your phone.

Camera images are never storedFrames are read in memory to find darts, then discarded. A paired phone sees a live preview that is never saved; nothing is uploaded.
No account neededPlaying, practice and tournaments need no internet. A second phone, board phones and the big screen only need your own Wi-Fi. An account is optional, for backup and for a match you played on a friend's phone.
Local by defaultThe second phone, board phones and the big screen talk to your phone over your own network, never through a server.
No ads, no trackingNo advertising, no analytics, no trackers. Not in the app, not on this site.
